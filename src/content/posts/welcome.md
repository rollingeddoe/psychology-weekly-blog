Psychology offers a way to ask better questions about everyday life. Why do relationships change? How do children learn? What shapes the way we see other people?

This website is a home for reading that research a little more closely. It begins with a simple format: a selection of studies, explained one at a time, with enough detail to understand both the findings and their limits.

## A study at a time

Each weekly digest will introduce the question a paper asks, the people or data it studies, and the main findings. The original English title and a reference will make it easy to find the source.

Some papers will speak to familiar experiences. Others will offer a useful challenge to an idea that seems obvious. They do not need to tell a single overarching story to be worth reading together.

## Keeping the context

The size of a study matters. So does its design, the population it covers, and the way a result was measured. A useful summary leaves room for these details without asking the reader to work through an entire paper first.

An association does not establish cause and effect. A promising intervention is not automatically a recommendation for everyone. Where findings are uncertain, the writing should reflect that uncertainty.

## The English edition

Psychology Weekly also takes shape as a Chinese-language digest. This English edition offers another way into the same reading project, with its own article archive and links to original research.

The first English research digest is still to come. For now, this is an introduction to the project and the approach behind it.
